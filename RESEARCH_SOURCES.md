# Sedlacek research: free sources to pull

Compiled 28 Sep 2026 from web searches. Most sites were blocked from this environment, so nearly every link below came from search results and has not been opened yet. Treat every fact here as a lead until you see the record yourself. Everything listed is free (FamilySearch needs a free account).

Confidence: **C** confirmed in a snippet, **L** likely, **S** speculative.

## New leads found

### Plattsmouth Sedlaks who might be related (all S)

- **Matej "Mike" Sedlak** (22 Dec 1878 – 4 Mar 1960), Holy Sepulchre, Plattsmouth; wife Katerina Capova. His memorial ID 22794845 is right next to Joseph's (22794841) and Anna's (22794840), so the same person likely added all three. He is the best candidate for a brother of Joseph (another son of James). https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/22794845
- **Thomas Sedlak**, about 1876–1943, a former Plattsmouth resident and Burlington railroad worker who died in Omaha. Plattsmouth Journal obituary, 30 Aug 1943. Another possible brother.
- Probably not this line: James Jacob Sedlak (1890–1983, came over in 1911), Frank E. Sedlak (1903–1992, born in Bohemia to Joseph and Marie Jozova).

### Where the family came from

- Holy Sepulchre cemetery transcription: "John Bukacek (b. Feb 24, 1849, d. Sep 23, 1928) born in **Tripove, Moravia**." So Anna Bukacek's side came from **Moravia**, not Bohemia. "Tripove" is a garbled spelling and the real village is not identified yet.
- A 1905 Plattsmouth obituary mentions a woman from "Racicich, Hrottovice, Znojemsky, Moravia," which is probably Račice near Hrotovice (now Třebíč district). That points to a Plattsmouth group from the Hrotovice area. It is not tied to our family yet.
- Nothing names a home village for James Sedlak or Mary Cobotka yet. "Cobotka/Kobotka" gets no hits in Czech surname data, so the spelling may be garbled (try Kobotek, Chobotka, Kopotka).

## Pull these first (ranked)

1. **Holy Sepulchre Cemetery transcription (USGenWeb):** http://files.usgwarchives.net/ne/cass/cemeteries/holysepul.txt. Plain text. Should list every Sedlak/Sedlacek buried in the Plattsmouth Catholic cemetery, probably including James (1890) and Mary, with birthplaces for some.
2. **Plattsmouth Journal, free full text (UNL Nebraska Newspapers):** https://nebnewspapers.unl.edu/lccn/2016270206/ (also sn95069723 for earlier years). Search "Sedlacek" and "Sedlak."
   - Oct 1929, possibly Anna's death notice: https://nebnewspapers.unl.edu/lccn/2016270206/1929-10-10/ed-1/seq-4/ocr/ and https://nebnewspapers.unl.edu/lccn/2016270206/1929-10-24/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/
   - 2 Jun 1932 Frances's wedding (lists family): https://nebnewspapers.unl.edu/lccn/2016270206/1932-06-02/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/
   - 1937, Joseph's obituary (survivor list should name all his children): search that year
   - 30 Aug 1943, Thomas Sedlak obituary
   - For James (d. 1890): Plattsmouth Weekly Herald, https://nebnewspapers.unl.edu/lccn/2016270200/
3. **FamilySearch censuses** (free login): search Joseph Sedlacek/Sedlak in the 1900 census in Grand Island (Hall Co.) and 1910/1920/1930 in Plattsmouth. Each lists all children living at home. Also try 1880 for James's household, which would list Joseph at about 9 and his brothers. 1900 census: https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/collection/1325221
4. **Find a Grave:** open Joseph (22794841), Anna (22794840), and Matej (22794845) and check the family links and photos. Browse Holy Sepulchre Cemetery (https://www.findagrave.com/cemetery/1959003) for surnames Sedlak and Sedlacek.
5. **Omaha Area Obits and Marriages indexes:** https://omahaobits.wordpress.com/se-sh/ and https://omahamarriages.wordpress.com/sd-sg/. These give dates and newspaper pages for every Omaha Sedlacek.
6. **Nebraska Death Index 1904–1968** (Reclaim the Records, free): https://nebraskadeathindex.com/. Gives exact dates for Anna (1929) and Joseph (1937).
7. **Cass County obituary indexes (NEGenWeb):** http://negenweb.net/NECass/deaths/obits5.htm (and obits1 through the others), http://negenweb.net/NECass/deaths/pjobit2.htm, and http://sites.rootsweb.com/~necass/pjobit4.htm
8. **Ancestry public genealogy pages.** These are free to view without a subscription:
   - Emil and Frank searches: https://www.ancestry.com/genealogy/records/results?firstName=emil&lastName=sedlacek
   - Johann Bukacek: https://www.ancestry.com/genealogy/records/johann-bukacek-24-5ymtlv

## Immigration and naturalization

- **Nebraska Naturalization Records 1855–1989** (FamilySearch, indexed): https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/collection/3241361. Pre-1906 records sometimes give an arrival date. Joseph's or James's papers would help most.
- **Czech immigrants database (Hospodář subscribers, 1890s, plus Rosický names), with towns of birth:** https://czecharchives.com/names-databases/czech-immigrants-in-usa. Check Sedlak, Sedlacek, Bukacek, Fousek.
- **Leo Baca, *Czech Immigration Passenger Lists*** (1830s–1890s): index at https://cgsi.org/leo-bacas-czech-immigration-passenger-lists. The volumes are on HathiTrust: https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/008369853
- **Castle Garden / New York 1820–1891:** FamilySearch, or Steve Morse's search at https://stevemorse.org/ellis/cg.html
- **Hamburg Passenger Lists 1850–1934** (free images on FamilySearch): https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Hamburg_Passenger_Lists
- **Rosický, *A History of Czechs (Bohemians) in Nebraska*** (1929): https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/006691947. Name index: https://sites.rootsweb.com/~nedodge/books/czecham.txt (A–M). It mentions a "Joseph Sedlacek, County Treasurer 1922–26," but it's unconfirmed which county, so it may not be our Joseph.

## Church records

Plattsmouth is in the **Diocese of Lincoln**, not Omaha. Holy Rosary (the Czech parish, founded 1891) and St. John the Baptist merged in 1971 into the **Church of the Holy Spirit**, which should hold both parishes' registers. Write to them for baptisms, marriages, and burials:
- https://holyspiritplattsmouth.com/about-us/
- https://www.lincolndiocese.org/directory/parishes/768-church-of-the-holy-spirit-plattsmouth

## Tools for finding the village

- **Surname maps:** kdejsme.cz and prijmeni.cz (e.g. https://www.kdejsme.cz/prijmeni/Huml%C3%AD%C4%8Dek/). Humlíček (about 150 people) and Holenda are the rarest names in the tree, so they narrow things down best. Look for a district where Bukáček, Humlíček, and Fousek overlap, probably in southwest Moravia given the Tripove and Hrotovice hints.
- **Czech Statistical Office historical lexicon of municipalities 1869–2015:** https://csu.gov.cz/produkty/historicky-lexikon-obci-1869-az-2015
- **Stable cadastre maps (1820s–40s):** https://archivnimapy.cuzk.cz
- **FamilySearch word lists for reading Czech, Latin, and German registers:** https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Czech_Genealogical_Word_List

## Free help

- **CGSI:** https://cgsi.org. Free guides; its databases are members only.
- **Czech Genealogy for Beginners** blog (http://czechgenealogy.nase-koreny.cz) and Facebook group (https://www.facebook.com/groups/czechgenealogy/). Good for reading hard entries and working out garbled village names like "Tripove."
