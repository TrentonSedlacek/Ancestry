## Plain full-list sources (no search box)

- **sortedbyname.com** (the same site as the Grand Island CSV). Its index (https://sortedbyname.com/sources/, checked Sep 2026) has only two Nebraska cemeteries: `USANE_GrandIsland` (done) and `US_NE_Kearney` (Kearney Cemetery, Buffalo Co., low priority). It also has national files that cover everyone, not just one cemetery:
  - **How to use NUMIDENT/SSDMF there:** sortedbyname.com has merged NUMIDENT and the Death Master File into its main A-to-Z name list. Go to https://sortedbyname.com , pick letter **S**, and page to the range that contains **SEDLACEK** (also SEDLAK, SEDLACK). That page is a plain list with birth and death dates, and often the birthplace and parents' names.
  - `USA_NUMIDENT.html` / `numident.html`: Social Security NUMIDENT. Gives birth date, **birthplace, and parents' names** for people who died after about 1936. This is the best chance to identify **Albert** and to confirm Bernard, Kenneth, and Edgar.
  - `USA_SSDMF.html`: the Social Security Death Master File (birth and death dates, last residence).
  - `BIRLS.html` and `USA_Veterans_Admin.html`: the VA death file (veterans' birth and death dates, service dates). For Edgar (Navy), Bernard, Kenneth, George Daniel, Emil.
  - `USANE_Prison.html`: Nebraska prison records (low priority).
- **North Platte / Lincoln County:** lincoln.negenealogy.org is dead (the domain no longer resolves). Try http://negenweb.net/NELincoln/cemeteries.html
- **Omaha Catholic cemeteries (Calvary, Holy Sepulchre, St. Mary's): no full list exists online.** interment.net has only 23 (Holy Sepulchre) and 6 (Calvary) records. The complete burial cards (1873–1990) and interment registers (1873–1940) are at History Nebraska, collection RG4014.AM (https://history.nebraska.gov/collection_section/catholic-cemeteries-omaha-neb-rg4014-am/), and on microfilm at the Omaha Public Library. Both are free, but you have to go in person or ask the archive.

## Why the first one worked

The Holy Sepulchre link worked because we had the **exact file address** (`files.usgwarchives.net/ne/cass/cemeteries/holysepul.txt`), with `https://web.archive.org/web/2020/` put in front of it. The Wayback Machine is reliable for exact addresses. Its wildcard listing pages (`/web/*/...`) are slow and often fail to load.

## Easiest way in: the Nebraska table of contents

1. Open **https://web.archive.org/web/2020/http://usgwarchives.net/ne/nefiles.htm**. This is the archived USGenWeb Nebraska page, with a link to every county.
2. Click a county (e.g. Hall, Douglas, Sherman). The Wayback Machine keeps you inside the archive as you click, so each county page lists its cemetery files, and each file opens as plain text like Holy Sepulchre did.
   - Sherman County's page, for example: https://web.archive.org/web/2020/http://usgwarchives.net/ne/sherman/shetable.htm
3. In each cemetery file, press Ctrl+F and search for `Sedl`. That catches Sedlacek, Sedlak, Sedlack, and Sedlock. Also try `Sidl`, and the related surnames `Bukacek`, `Bucacek`, `Korinek`, `Koubek`, `Klinge`, `Delisle`, `Kamper`, `Rozic`.
4. Paste the matching rows, or the whole file, into the chat.

If a click lands on "not archived," change the year in the address (e.g. `/web/2018/` or `/web/2022/`).
